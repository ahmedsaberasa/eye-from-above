02 • Radar Physics Made Simple

How NASA Radar Sees Water Underneath Storm Clouds.

Instead of taking a regular camera photo, NASA's NISAR satellite sends invisible microwave pulses (24 cm L-band) through clouds and measures the echo (backscatter) that bounces back.

🪞 Physical Driver 1: Specular Reflection (Dark Water)

Calm floodwater has a high dielectric constant and is smooth like a mirror. Radar waves bounce away from the satellite, creating sharp dark zones (< -17.5 dB).

🌿 Physical Driver 2: Rough Dry Ground & Vegetation

Rough dry soil, trees, and buildings scatter radar energy back to space, appearing bright gray. Longer L-band waves even penetrate crop canopies.

🚤 Shallow Flood vs. Deep Boat-Only Currents

Comparing Aug 19 (Before) and Aug 31 (Peak Flood) isolates 35.86 km² of flooding and separates shallow farms from deep water where only boats can enter.

07 • Complete Mission & Executive Summary Dancing with the SARs
The Challenge

Monsoon storm clouds blind optical satellites for days during floods, while raw Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) imagery is too complex for field responders to turn into fast rescue routes and supply plans.

Our Solution: Eye from Above

An end-to-end disaster intelligence platform that converts all-weather NASA NISAR L-Band radar into priority village rankings, AI-scored safe dry corridors, predictive flood simulations, and offline field kits.

How We Solved It & Core Features
1. NISAR Radar Physics: Compares Aug 19 vs. Aug 31 scans (< -17.5 dB specular dark water) to map 35.86 km² of shallow & deep boat-only water.
2. Village & Road Fusion: Fuses OpenStreetMap to rank 5 trapped villages and prune 24 submerged roads from the routing graph.
3. MCDA AI & +250m Sim: Scores 5 rescue bases (top: 86.8/100) and auto-relocates the Boat Drop-Off point when water rises.
4. Sphere & Offline Kit: Calculates 72h water/meals/tents/boats and exports .html, .geojson, and .md files for zero-internet teams.
